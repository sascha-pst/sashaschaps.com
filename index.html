hy·phae /ˈhaɪ-fee/ pl. noun

  1. mycology. The study of fungi. Adamatzky (2022) recorded electrical spikes travelling those filaments in trains structured enough to analyze like a language. The popular "wood-wide web," though, is contested (Karst et al. 2023) — a standing lesson in overinterpreted effect sizes.
  2. Bay Area. See hyphy: a heightened, highly productive state of hyperactivity — native to the Bay.
  3. neuroplasticity. The brain rewires with use; such that in the years of working on brain rot, there can be new growth with new training data. In computational neuroscience, the brain's dopamine system operated very similarly to TD learning in reinforcement learning.
  • Selected reading
  • Tero, A. et al. (2010). Rules for biologically inspired adaptive network design. Science 327 — a slime mould rebuilds the Tokyo rail map.
  • Adamatzky, A. (2022). Language of fungi derived from their electrical spiking activity. R. Soc. Open Sci. 9.
  • Dabney, W. et al. (2020). A distributional code for value in dopamine-based reinforcement learning. Nature 577.
  • Maguire, E. A. et al. (2000). Navigation-related structural change in the hippocampi of taxi drivers. PNAS 97.
  • Karst, J., Jones, M. D. & Hoeksema, J. D. (2023). Positive citation bias and overinterpreted results lead to misinformation on common mycorrhizal networks in forests. Nat. Ecol. Evol. 7.
