Whitepaper series · Online safety & community engagement

Promoting online safety.

Four whitepapers for the people who have to say something when a community starts to come apart.

A field set for holding a community together while it argues.

Written for the people who run the rooms — moderators, organisers, volunteer leads, community managers. Each paper takes a moment where trust is about to break and answers a single question: what do you actually say next?

  • Role — research, writing, facilitation design
  • Output — 4 whitepapers + workshop programme
  • For — community builders
Still one conversation Now a crisis

  1. Before it spreads

    Diffusing crucial conversations

    The exchange where someone first feels unheard is the one that turns into everything further along this line.

    • Make it safe to disagree out loud, so disagreement doesn’t go private
    • Separate the person from the position they arrived with
    • Ask what they heard before you tell them what’s true

    ReflexThat’s not what the evidence says.

    PractisedWhat’s making you doubt it? I want the version you were told.

  2. As it travels

    Stopping misinformation

    Repeating a rumour in order to correct it can carry it further than saying nothing at all. Correction is a design problem.

    • Lead with the true claim; never put the myth in the headline
    • Teach the technique, not just the claim — people who spot the move stay immune
    • Fill the vacuum early; silence gets written over by someone else
    Show the exchange Hide the exchange

    ReflexIt’s false that this causes harm — here’s the debunk.

    PractisedHere’s what it does. Here’s the trick being used on you. Here’s where to check it yourself.

  3. When it splits the room

    Conflict resolution

    Most community conflict is a fight about standing and voice wearing the costume of the item on the agenda.

    • Find the interest sitting underneath the demand
    • Trade positions for constraints — constraints can be shared, positions can’t
    • Let the group set the terms of repair, or repair won’t hold
    Show the exchange Hide the exchange

    ReflexLet’s keep this on topic and take it offline.

    PractisedTell me what you’d lose if we went the other way. That’s the part we haven’t discussed.

  4. When it breaks

    Responding to crisis

    In the first hour, early and honest beats complete. Credibility is spent in that hour and earned back over months.

    • Say what you know, what you don’t, and when you’ll be back
    • Announce the uncertainty before someone else discovers it
    • One message, many messengers — trusted local voices, not one podium
    Show the exchange Hide the exchange

    ReflexWe’ll comment once we have all the facts.

    PractisedHere’s what we know at 4pm. Here’s what we don’t. We’ll update at 8, even if nothing’s changed.
