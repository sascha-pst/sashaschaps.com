My dog Ebi came home from the groomer looking like a child from a 1990s class photo. Specifically, she looked like me from a 1990s class photo — bowl-cut fringe, ears exposed, an expression of mild bewilderment that I recognized as hereditary.

I had not asked for this. I had said "just a trim." The groomer, a woman of few words and strong opinions, had interpreted this as license.

The thing about Ebi is that she has never, in four years of life, looked undignified. She is a small dog with large ideas about herself. She navigates the world with the confidence of someone who has never been told she is a small dog. The bowl cut changed something. Not for her — she went immediately to her water bowl and then to the couch, unbothered — but for me.

I spent the rest of the afternoon sitting across from her, trying to figure out what I was actually thinking about.
