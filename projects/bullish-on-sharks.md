## The premise

Sharks have a perception problem that data does not support. Global shark attack statistics, when examined against the backdrop of ocean visitation rates and shark population trends, tell a more complicated story than the one that gets coverage.

This project started as a personal research exercise — context for diving with sharks, which I do — and turned into a more systematic look at where the numbers and the narrative diverge.

## What I looked at

- ISAF (International Shark Attack File) data on unprovoked encounters by year, region, and species
- IUCN Red List status for the most-encountered shark species
- Estimated ocean visitation growth versus encounter rate trends
- Media coverage frequency against encounter data (the coverage-to-incident ratio)

## A note on methodology

This is exploratory, not peer-reviewed. The encounter data has known collection biases — underreporting in some regions, inconsistent classification across years. I've tried to note where those limitations affect the conclusions.
